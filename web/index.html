01 / where this came from

It started with a fly brain.

01
A fly's whole brain has been mapped, wire by wire. Google Research built the segmentation behind it.
02
One circuit in there does a single job. Two cell types watch for something growing in the eye and fire one neuron. The fly is gone.
03
It never figures out what the thing is. It only measures how fast the edge spreads. So it cannot be fooled by a world that just looks right.
04
That makes it a physics test. Put it in an AI-generated world and it fires on time if the motion is real, and stays silent if it is nonsense.
05
Reactor is the other half. A world you can fly through live, so we send the fly's escape back into the session that made the frame.
02 / the game

So we made you the fly.

You walk down through A24's Backrooms, one AI-generated room at a time, and you have to survive them with a fly's senses and nothing else.

01
You see 223 facets and no detail. The whole game is rendered through the retina itself, at the measured 5.1 degree spacing, with no red because a fly has no receptor for it. You cannot tell what is in the room with you, because the fly cannot either.
02
Then one neuron fires and you get six seconds. Dodge with the arrow keys, or turn the camera on and physically swing your hand. A voice tells you to ignore the alarm, because staying is the only way to learn anything about the room.
03
One room kills you, and the reflex never fires. Why it stays quiet is the whole point of the project.

05 / the real world

What this is for.

AI worlds are about to be where robots learn. Nobody can tell you yet whether an update to one changed how a robot behaves inside it.

01
Robots trained on fake physics fail on real physics. If approaches arrive late in the sim, the robot brakes late in the street.
02
This gives a world model a pass or fail. Two builds, two scores, and the regression has a name.
03
Self-driving sims get checked on the thing that matters. Time to contact is what a car brakes on, and it is exactly what this measures.
04
It is cheap enough to run on every commit. No judge model, no per-frame bill, just numpy on a laptop.
05
The number comes from an animal, not from us. Which is why anyone can check it.
06
Evolution already paid the training bill. No data, no labels, no compute. There are more instruments like this in the connectome that nobody has picked up.
